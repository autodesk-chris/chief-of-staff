## Summary

**Overall assessment:** Good first strategy unit strategy with solid data foundation and sound pillars. Four areas need sharpening to make this a strong, actionable strategy.

**Priority feedback (what I'll cover in the QAM):**

1. **Unit-level thinking, not squad-level.** The strategy reads as First Strike's strategy with other squads mentioned as future partners. While there was limited contribution from other Squads, I think it still should be framed from the perspective of what needs to happen to drive growth in the next 6 months, versus how do we make onboarding better. 
2. **Confront the product gaps question.** Even shared current interventions by the First Strike Squad stabilise activation rates but struggle to increase them. The goals are substantial and I question whether they are achievable without addressing feature gaps. Some are being addressed by longer term initiatives, but others are not. Other squads don't have bandwidth so I'd like to see Growth address in a targeted way - being accountable for the outcome means being willing to do what's needed.
3. **Sharpen destination.** The destination is aspirational but not measurable within the strategy's timeframe. I can't tell in 6 months whether we succeeded. Metrics repeat squad KRs rather than laddering up to unit-level outcomes (retention, 7-day engagement, cross-app activation).
4. **Get specific about where to win - focus on US.** The strategy says US and UK are target geos but experiments will be geo-agnostic. That's a contradiction. The US is our largest new-user market and hardest growth challenge. What would change about the strategy if the explicit goal was to win in the US first? Different use cases, different jobs to be done, different feature gaps. If we solve for the US, tactics likely transfer. If we solve for everyone equally, we solve for no one deeply enough.
5. **General.** I'd like to see a more succinct and focused document to make it faster and easier to digest without losing the important points - there is a lot of strategies for the group to cover. For each section, ask (1) does this section do what the template asks for, and only that? (2) Would someone with no context understand this clearly in one read?

## Detailed review

### Context
- Author: Even Olstad (First Strike squad lead, strategy unit driver)
- Status: Forming. First strategy unit strategy for Even (has done squad strategies before)
- Even framed this as a dry run - learning opportunity. Coaching feedback, not assessment.
- Deadline: 2026-03-20 for QAM
- RACI: First Strike (R), Site Design / Engage / Analysis (A). Site Design and Analysis have limited bandwidth until H2.

### Strategy structure and template adherence

The strategy document template asks for specific things in each section. In several places, Even mixes fact with justification, or drifts between sections' purposes. For example, problem statements include explanations of why problems exist (which is a separate section). The mission doesn't clearly state why the strategy exists - it describes what the team will do instead. Key capabilities use internal jargon ("outcome taxonomy", "consistent guidance surface") that wouldn't be clear to someone outside the team.

**Coaching point:** The template is a thinking tool. Each section has a specific job. When content bleeds between sections, it usually means the thinking hasn't been fully separated yet. A disciplined pass through the template, section by section, will sharpen the whole document.

### Squad vs strategy unit framing

This is the most important structural issue. Throughout:
- Bets section: "Squad First Strike, working on Site Design 1.0" repeated for Now, Next, and Later
- Metrics: Squad-level KRs (day-1 site entry, day-1 analysis) rather than unit-level outcomes
- Trunk problem: Framed for First Strike scope, not the unit
- Roles section: Other squads described as informing/partnering, not owning outcomes
- Operational metrics about "squads other than First Strike sharing learnings" - this frames the unit as First Strike plus observers

Even likely wrote it this way because other squads didn't fully engage in preparation. But the strategy should still describe the full picture and each squad's contribution, even if some are small or deferred to H2.

**What needs to change:**
- Trunk problem: Frame for the whole unit's scope (getting users to successful first value across Forma Design, not just site entry)
- Metrics: Unit-level outcomes - retention, 7-day engagement, cross-app activation rates
- Bets: Show what each squad is doing, even if it's "maintaining readiness" or "sharing learnings from X"
- Destination: What does success look like for the unit, not just for First Strike's experimentation

### Product gaps - the elephant in the room

Even explicitly scopes out product gaps ("feature bundles too large to handle in this timeframe"). But his own data tells a different story:
- Growth interventions stabilise the ratio of users who activate but don't meaningfully increase it
- Known feature gaps (e.g. US users can't complete a capacity study) have been stable and unaddressed for years
- Squads who'd normally fix them (Site Design, Analysis) don't have bandwidth until H2
- The strategy's ambitious metrics (70% site entry, 50% first strike) may have a ceiling imposed by these gaps

**The question to guide Even toward:** If feature gaps are the ceiling on outcomes, and no one else is addressing them in the near term, what should the strategy unit do? Options include:
- First Strike picks up targeted, high-impact feature work (e.g. enabling US capacity studies)
- The strategy explicitly acknowledges a lower outcome ceiling and adjusts targets
- The unit escalates for dedicated resourcing

This connects to a broader coaching point: Even tends to defer ("it's someone else's role"). Being the strategy unit driver means being accountable for the outcome. If the outcome requires work that traditionally belongs elsewhere, and that team can't do it, the driver needs to step up or escalate - not scope it out.

**4th pillar suggestion:** Consider adding a pillar around "closing critical jobs-to-be-done gaps" alongside Profiling, Onboarding, and Learning. The work Daniel did enabling US users to complete a capacity study is a good example - system-level value that directly drives first strike.

### Destination and metrics

**Destination issues:**
- Aspirational but not measurable ("reusable, outcome-driven experience system")
- Not time-bound - can't tell in 6 months if we achieved it
- Limited to onboarding experience, doesn't include users being able to complete specific jobs to be done
- The "end state" bullet points are good but need metrics attached

**Metrics issues:**
- Product metrics are all day-1, but the strategy covers the first week experience
- No post-day-1 metrics despite "1st week activity" being mentioned in the mission
- Metrics are squad KRs, not unit-level outcomes
- Operational metrics feel disconnected - "3 mechanisms adopted by 2+ squads" doesn't connect to user outcomes
- No baselines on several KRs (X to Y placeholders remain)
- No kill criteria defined

**What good looks like:** "By end of Q2, 50% of US commercial new users complete a meaningful analysis workflow within 7 days of first entry, up from X% baseline" - specific, measurable, time-bound, unit-scoped.

### Geo strategy

The strategy states US and UK as target geos but then says experiments will be geo-agnostic. This contradiction avoids making a hard choice.

**Why US focus matters:**
- Largest new-user market
- Hardest growth challenge
- US-specific use cases differ from Nordic ones (e.g. what "analysis" means, what contextual data is needed)
- Data quality may be stronger in US, reducing one variable
- If we solve for the US, tactics likely transfer elsewhere
- UK data limitations may mean we should sequence US first anyway

**Question for Even:** What would change about the problem statements, bets, and success metrics if the explicit goal was to win in the US market first?

### Problem spaces

- Trunk problem ("Value perception gap") is reasonable but framed for First Strike's scope
- 6 branch problems listed - too many. Template asks for 2-3. Which will have most impact on outcomes?
- Problem statements mix observable behaviour with assumed motivation in places
- "Product gaps" listed as a branch problem but then scoped out of the strategy - this is contradictory
- Problems should focus on the US market given it's the key geo and largest new-user cohort
- Would benefit from being more specific - e.g. "US architects can't complete a capacity study" vs generic "product gaps"

### Mission

The mission describes what the team will do ("turn new users into returning users through tailored experience") rather than why the strategy exists and the enduring value it creates. The template asks for purpose, not activity.

### Customer value

Written reasonably well but could be more concrete. "Tailored experience guiding them to a successful, personalized value moment" is still somewhat generic. What specific outcome does a US architect walk away with after their first session? Being concrete here helps everything downstream.

### Solution spaces and bets

- Key capabilities use jargon that needs translation for broader audience
- Bet solution spaces written for First Strike specifically ("partner with product squads") rather than the unit
- Bets are all First Strike-focused. Other squads need representation.
- A lot of ground covered in Now phase - 6 bullet points across profiling, onboarding, learning, research, and ADSK initiatives. Is this realistic for one squad?
- Bets don't clearly map back to branch problems. Which bet addresses which problem?
- Feature gap work is entirely absent from bets
- Immediate bets: 5 items listed where template asks for 2-3. What's the priority order?

### Risks and dependencies

- Good identification of risks
- Mitigation for low user numbers: should explicitly state "make decisions on 'more likely than not' basis rather than waiting for statistical significance" and leverage qualitative interviews alongside quantitative
- Missing the biggest risk: that outcomes have a ceiling imposed by product gaps that aren't being addressed
- Strategy unit mandate question is legitimate - we need to work this out together

### Outstanding questions from Even

Even asks several important questions that I need to engage with:
- Strategy unit definition and mandate - no pre-baked answer, we co-define this. But as driver, he's accountable for driving the outcome across the group.
- Expected contribution from Site Design to FY27 MAU targets - need to provide clarity
- Product gaps (long vs short term view) - his own data makes the case. Guide him to the conclusion.

### Writing quality

The document is thorough but could be more succinct. Several sections have repetitive content. Some sentences require multiple reads. A editing pass focused on "one idea per sentence, cut every unnecessary word" would help significantly. The appendix (moats list, DACI explanation, RACI definition) adds length without adding strategic value - consider removing or linking to references instead.
