Product Center CTA:

Prediction: If we improve the primary CTA language on the Forma Site Design PC, we expect to see an increase in the conversion rate from visitors with AEC entitlements and a reduction in distractions among visitors without AEC entitlements. - PC CTA experiment (Live and pending results) - [Final results](https://share.autodesk.com/:p:/s/marketingoptimization/IQALidq2RHGIQpK7fGlW24Y1AfKurDmbe7UhI5uAMieHuLA?e=y48Mec)

Status: Completed

Results: The experiment did not show a clear improvement over the Control CTA. Updating the CTA to “Log in to Forma Site Design” resulted in lower engagement with the primary CTA across multiple segments, most notably among Active Forma entitlement users, where the decline was statistically significant.

The intended increase in engagement among AEC-C–entitled users was not observed, and results for this segment remain inconclusive due to very small sample sizes. Among users without entitlements, the Challenger showed a modest reduction in primary CTA clicks, though this effect was not statistically strong.

At the same time, the data suggests the new CTA helped reduce engagement with less relevant secondary CTAs for entitled users, particularly “See pricing”, where a statistically significant decline was observed among Active Forma users. This indicates that the revised wording may have improved clarity around access status and discouraged unnecessary pricing exploration.

Overall, while the Challenger shows a potential benefit in suppressing irrelevant secondary actions, this came with a clear trade-off in primary CTA engagement. On balance, the data supports continuing with the Control CTA.
