
# **Purpose**

The Forma Design operating model exists to help us work with intent and deliver stronger outcomes for our users and the business.

It connects strategy, investment, and execution into a single system so that teams move in the same direction, adapt quickly as reality changes, and maximise the value of our time and resources.

The goal is alignment without rigidity. Squads retain empowerment in how they work, while the organization stays connected, focused, and able to make high quality decisions quickly.

This model is designed to:

- Align teams around shared outcomes
- Focus investment on what matters most
- Replace planning friction with shared intelligence
- Enable both us to make better decisions

This operating model applies to all teams across Forma Design. It is expected to evolve over time. Done and improving is better than perfect.

# **Guiding Principles**

These principles are not values statements. They are constraints on how we make decisions and how we work together.

### **One team, one model**

- All squads across all product areas operate within the same framework.
- We use shared OKRs, shared strategies, shared bets, and shared cadences.
- We succeed or fail as one system, not as isolated teams or functions.

### **Outcomes over outputs**

- Success is defined by measurable changes in user behaviour that drive business impact.
- Shipping work is not an outcome.
- Changes in usage, adoption, and value creation are outcomes.
- User action is the source of truth - optimise towards user-experienced behaviours and experimentation over research-based or subjective decision-making.

### **Data-informed, user centered**

- Decisions are guided by quantitative and qualitative data.
- Success must be defined in measurable terms wherever possible.

### **Transparency builds trust**

- We operate openly by default.
- Strategies, bets, learnings, and data are visible across squads. Transparency accelerates learning, improves alignment, and strengthens trust.

### **Deliberate collaboration in ambiguity**

- Ambiguity is normal.
- When boundaries blur, we engage early, stay curious, and align through shared rituals. The goal is clarity of purpose and ownership, not rigid separation.

# **Operating Model – 4 Pillars**

The operating model has four pillars. Together they describe how work flows from intent to impact.

1. OKRs define where we are going
2. Strategies define how we will win
3. Bets define what we are doing and will do next
4. Execution defines how we deliver together
## **OKRs**

OKRs define the shared destination for Forma Design. They align squads, product areas, and leadership around the same outcomes.

- Leadership defines a set of shared OKRs for Forma Design
- Every Objective and Key Result has a ~~clear owner~~ main driver
- OKRs anchor strategy definition, prioritisation, and people allocation
- Progress against OKRs is reviewed regularly and transparently

## **Strategies**

- Strategies define how we solve the most important problems tied to the OKRs.
- A strategy exists to address a meaningful problem, not to deliver a predefined solution.

#### Strategy structure

- Strategies should typically follow the same format for easy consumption
- Each strategy starts with a trunk problem.
- Branch problems deepen understanding of the trunk problem and break it into solvable parts.
- When a branch problem is large or complex enough, it may become a sub strategy with its own plan and ownership.

#### Strategy ownership and funding

- Anyone can propose a strategy
- Leadership decides which strategies are funded each quarter
- Only funded strategies receive squad and people allocation
- Squads are assigned based on skills and fit, not organizational boundaries

Strategies are reviewed regularly. Squads iterate on strategy details as insight emerges. Leadership checkpoints ensure changes remain aligned with funded intent and portfolio tradeoffs.

All strategies are documented in the Strategy repository.

## **Bets**

- Bets are the tactical actions squads take to deliver on strategies.
- Together, bets form the execution roadmap for a strategy.

#### What defines a bet

- A bet delivers a usable outcome on its own
- A bet is sized to complete within a single six week cycle
- Bets longer than six weeks are split
- Every bet is linked to specific Key Results

Bets must not assume automatic continuation into the next cycle.

#### Bet types

There are two types of bets

- Experiments, used when learning and validation are required
- Initiatives, used when confidence is already high

Bets may begin as ideas and are refined before delivery.

### **Ownership and commitment**

- Anyone can propose a bet
- Squads decide which bets they commit to
- Execution is owned by the squad best suited to deliver, not necessarily the proposing squad

All bets use a consistent format and are documented in the Bets repository.

## **Execution**

- Execution is how squads work together to deliver strategies.
- Squads assigned to the same strategy form a strategy unit.

#### **How execution works**

- Strategy units collaborate to deliver shared outcomes
- Squads retain autonomy in how they execute their bets
- Squads share insights, learnings, and resources by default
- Progress is communicated collectively at the strategy level

Execution autonomy exists within clear strategic alignment.

#### **Decision Rights**

Clear decision rights are essential to move fast without confusion.

- Squads decide how to deliver their bets
- Strategy units decide tradeoffs within a funded strategy
- Leadership decides portfolio priorities, funding, and people allocation

If a decision crosses these boundaries, escalation should happen early.

# **Planning and Execution Rituals**

- Rituals exist to enable decisions, not to create process or reporting for reporting sake.
- Product Area Leads may meet with their squads in addition to the rituals above.

## **Quarterly Strategic Alignment Meeting**

**Purpose**

Align on progress against OKRs, validate strategies, and set quarterly focus.

**Desired Outcome**

Shared understanding, clear priorities, and confirmed targets for everyone in Forma Design.

**Agenda**

- **Metrics:** Review progress and status of OKRs and key metrics.
- **General Review:** Reflect on what worked, what didn’t, and what should change.
- **Strategy Review:** Assess whether the strategy holds, adjustments needed, and whether we have the right squads and capabilities assigned.
- **Quarterly Focus:** Identify one or two top focus areas for the quarter and define the metrics that represent success.
- **Clarify Targets:** Confirm the OKRs / targets for the quarter.

**Preparation**

Update the strategy and capture progress against relevant KRs. Provide a pre-read at least 5 days in advance.

**Attendees**

Squad PMs / Leads working on the strategy, Product Area leadership, VP Forma Design, Director XA, Director Strategy, Director Engineering, others as relevant.

**Logistics**

Quarterly, ~2.5 hours. Strategy unit shares outputs within 48 hours. The Product Leadership group will select which strategy units attend each quarter based on priorities.

## **Quarterly Strategy Funding Meeting**

**Purpose**

Align on which strategies will be funded (new or existing).

**Desired Outcome**

Clear investment decisions and strategic alignment.

**Agenda**

- **Review current strategy outcomes:** Brief recap of progress, learnings, and effectiveness from the quarterly strategy meetings.
- **Strategy prioritisation discussion:** Review and evaluate strategies against leadership priorities, OKRs, capacity, and expected ROI.
- **Funding decisions:** Confirm which strategies will be funded for the next quarter and relevant Squad/people allocation. Note any changes requiring immediate action.
- **Next steps:** Capture decisions and communicate to squads, and outline any follow-up actions.
    

**Preparation:** Strategy documents provided by Strategy units. Existing strategies updated with short reason for continue or stop decision. New strategies drafted

**Attendees:** VP Forma Design, Product Area leadership, Director Engineering, Director Strategy, Director XA.

**Logistics:** First week of each quarter, ~a day session.

## **~~Leadership~~ Monthly Focus Meetings (MFM)**

**Purpose**

Provide space for squads and leadership to examine progress and decide what to do next.

**Desired Outcome**

Clear monthly focus, confidence in quarterly targets, and early identification of issues.

**Agenda**

- **Metrics:** Review the key metrics for strategy units and squads; surface issues.
- **Review:** Reflect on the previous month’s focus areas and bets. Assess confidence in hitting quarterly targets.
- **Monthly focus:** Identify the top one or two focus areas for the coming month.
- **Bet review:** Align on the Bets that will have the most impact on delivering the monthly focus and should be prioritised
- **Issues & asks:** Identify support needed or blockers to resolve.

**Preparation**

Updated metrics and status, progress on previous focus areas and bets, recommendation for next month’s focus and bets.

**Attendees**

Strategy unit Squad PMs / Leads, Product Area leadership, others as relevant.

**Logistics**

First Monday and Tuesday of each month. ~2 hours. Strategy unit shares outputs within 48 hours. [https://autodesk.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/fdo/pages/659068251](https://autodesk.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/fdo/pages/659068251)

## **Strategy Unit + Squad planning**

**Purpose**

Ensure cross-squad visibility into sprint priorities and surface dependencies early.

**Desired Outcome**

Aligned sprint priorities and clarity on cross-squad asks.

**Agenda**

- **Priorities:** Squads present top priorities for the upcoming sprint & key deliverables
- **Asks & dependencies:** Squads identify key asks and dependencies
- **Next steps:** Confirm next steps and any relevant action

**Attendees**

Squad PMs / Leads, Engineering Lead, XA Lead working on a strategy.

**Logistics**

Once per sprint; ~30 minutes.

**Notes**

Squads decide how they want to run the session. Individual squads continue to run their own rituals.
