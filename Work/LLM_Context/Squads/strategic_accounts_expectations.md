# The destination

## Why we're doing this

Enterprise accounts will be an important future target user group. However, they have higher expectations for a product to fit their existing workflows - and our solution isn't yet mature enough for most large account users. The opportunity with the current offering is smaller than we'd like.

That said, some enterprises have adopted Forma, and we want to engage these users with an eye on the future. What we've learned is that hands-on user success support is critical - there are specific tactics that drive adoption that can't be left to chance.

As we think about monetizing larger accounts, we face a capability gap: our new Sales team and their leaders, along with our existing CS team, don't yet have the depth of understanding needed to both sell effectively and ensure adoption stays high enough that users want to keep using the product.

Strategic Accounts exists to close that gap - building the hands-on expertise now, proving what works with early adopters, and enabling Sales and CS to execute when the product matures and enterprise becomes a bigger opportunity.

## What success looks like

- **Account growth at target levels**: 6 accounts with 100+ users; 6 additional accounts with 50+ users; at least 2 of these accounts in APAC
- **Sales teams confident and capable**: Rating their product understanding 4/5 or higher, confident presenting all aspects of Forma, and able to talk through the adoption playbook without needing specialist support
- **A clear, aligned strategy**: A documented approach to supporting Sales and CS that's been developed collaboratively with their leadership (using the Confluence strategy template)
- **Enterprise needs on product roadmaps**: Critical enterprise blockers (ACC/Docs integration, admin constraints, coordinate workflows) tracked and progressing with product teams

## The focus

- **Enable, don't do**: The goal is Sales and CS self-sufficiency. Hands-on account work is for learning and proving approaches - not a permanent operating model
- **Playbooks for different contexts**: Large accounts aren't all the same. Build playbooks that account for company size, internal setup, geography, and champion structures
- **Advisory, not directive**: When engaging with accounts, operate as advisors to Sales/CS rather than running engagement activities directly
- **Enterprise voice to product**: Be the conduit for enterprise requirements into product roadmaps - helping shape the maturity needed for broader enterprise adoption

## How I expect you to work

**High autonomy, aligned direction**: You drive the strategy and stakeholder relationships. I want input into direction, but you and the team own execution. Coordinate closely with Sales and CS leadership - but remember you're the experts on enterprise adoption. Stakeholders should recognize that expertise.

**Consultative stance**: Your relationship with Sales and CS is advisory. Help them succeed rather than doing it for them. When they default to requesting specialist support, that's a signal the enablement isn't working yet.

**Can't operate in isolation**: Enterprise feedback needs to reach product teams through a unified channel. Coordinate with User Engagement and product squads to create a single approach - don't build a parallel feedback loop.

**Balance hands-on and scalable**: For the next 6 months, expect roughly 40-50% hands-on account work and 50-60% enablement of Sales/CS. The hands-on work generates insights; the enablement work scales them.

## My role

- Align on strategic direction and provide input
- Help clear blockers with product teams on enterprise requirements
- Connect you with leadership when needed
- Challenge whether the balance is right between hands-on and enablement

# Detailed expectations

### Build the enterprise adoption playbook

**What**: Develop documented playbooks for driving adoption at large accounts, accounting for different contexts and scenarios.

**Key activities**:
- Define playbook variants for different company sizes, internal setups, and geographies
- Document what works (and doesn't) from hands-on account engagement
- Include champion identification, network building, and leadership air-cover strategies
- Create clear criteria for when accounts are on/off track (license use, first strike rates)

**Expectations**: The playbook should be specific enough that CS can execute it without your team's involvement. Vague guidance like "build relationships" isn't a playbook - concrete steps, triggers, and responses are.

### Enable Sales teams on product and playbook

**What**: Get Sales teams to a point where they're confident and capable presenting Forma and the adoption approach independently.

**Key activities**:
- Run regular training sessions (weekly cadence)
- Build self-serve help resources tailored to playbook execution
- Work with Sales leadership to ensure training lands and sticks
- Measure confidence through surveys (product knowledge, demo comfort, playbook confidence)

**Expectations**: Target is 75% of Sales/CS rating their confidence 4/5 or higher. If teams still default to requesting specialist support after training, diagnose why and adjust the approach.

### Enable CS teams on adoption support

**What**: Equip CS to implement the adoption playbook effectively with purchased accounts.

**Key activities**:
- Work with CS leadership to integrate playbook into their operating rhythm
- Train CS on handling user questions and driving engagement
- Build self-serve resources for common scenarios
- Define clear handover points between Sales, CS, and Strategic Accounts

**Expectations**: CS should be the primary driver of post-sale adoption, with Strategic Accounts as advisors. The April Oslo workshop with Ken should produce a clear collaboration framework with defined handover points.

### Advise on strategic accounts (hands-on learning)

**What**: Engage directly with key accounts to learn what works and prove the playbook.

**Key activities**:
- Maintain hands-on involvement with priority accounts (Jacobs, Arcadis, AECOM, Sweco, Foster+Partners, Stantec)
- Operate as advisor to Sales/CS rather than running activities directly
- Document learnings and feed them back into playbook development
- Track account growth toward targets (6 accounts at 100+, 6 at 50+, 2+ in APAC)

**Expectations**: Direct engagement is a learning mechanism, not the end state. Every account interaction should generate insight that improves the playbook or training. The BDP champion model experiment (103% uplift) is the kind of learning we need more of.

### Get enterprise requirements onto product roadmaps

**What**: Ensure critical enterprise blockers are visible to product teams and tracked through to execution.

**Key activities**:
- Identify enterprise-critical product gaps (ACC/Docs integration, admin constraints, coordinate workflows)
- Find an approach to surface these to product teams effectively
- Track whether requirements are getting on roadmaps and being executed
- Coordinate with User Engagement and product teams for a unified feedback approach

**Expectations**: Don't build a parallel feedback channel. Work with existing squads to create one approach to user feedback. But make sure enterprise voice is heard - these accounts have different needs than individual users, and their feedback shapes the product maturity needed for broader enterprise adoption.

### Develop and align on strategy

**What**: Create a clear strategic document aligned with Sales and CS leadership.

**Key activities**:
- Draft strategy using the Confluence strategy template
- Align with Sales and CS leadership on approach and priorities
- Define success metrics and tracking mechanisms
- Review and iterate based on what's working

**Expectations**: Strategy should be developed collaboratively with stakeholders, not handed down. You're the expert - but Sales and CS need to own execution, so they need to shape the approach.

# Operating principles

1. **Enable over do**: Every time you do something for Sales/CS, ask whether you could teach them to do it instead.

2. **Hands-on serves scale**: Direct account work is for learning. If you're not extracting insights that improve the playbook, you're just doing consulting.

3. **Expertise earns authority**: You're the experts on enterprise adoption. Make sure stakeholders recognize that - but use it to advise, not to dictate.

4. **One feedback channel**: Don't operate in isolation. Enterprise requirements flow through the same system as other user feedback.

5. **Build for the future**: Today's enterprise engagement shapes tomorrow's opportunity. The insights and relationships built now pay off when the product matures.
