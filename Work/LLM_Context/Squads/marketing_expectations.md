# The destination

## Why we're doing this

Forma is about outcome-based design - helping users compare options and achieve the outcomes they want. But that's not the narrative in the market. Users come expecting another design tool like Rhino or SketchUp, which sets them up for disappointment. We need to own the conversation around outcome-based design and ensure users arrive with the right expectations.

Marketing's job is to build that narrative and make it stick. This means creating a powerful content engine that tells the stories of users getting value from Forma, developing messaging that's opinionated about what we do (and don't do), and activating channels that amplify our voice at scale.

The opportunity is significant: if we own share of voice around outcome-based design, we don't just generate leads - we shape how the market understands this category. Users will come to Forma knowing what it's for, which means higher activation, better retention, and advocates who spread the message for us.

## What success looks like

- **Own share of voice on outcome-based design**: Forma is synonymous with outcome-based design in the AEC market. When people talk about comparing design options to achieve outcomes, they talk about Forma
- **Community as our #1 growth channel**: Users are our primary voices. They're sharing content, representing us in forums, telling stories about what Forma does for them
- **Product Center challenging for #1**: Owned content driving significant inbound traffic and self-serve leads
- **Feature development as a GTM channel**: Product launches are content moments that current and potential users want to hear about
- **Self-serve leads at scale**: Content, events, and platforms (podcasts, etc.) driving leads to self-serve channels
- **Stakeholders aligned on PLG requirements**: Product and other teams understand what's needed for self-serve success and are supporting the operating model

## The focus

- **Messaging that takes a position**: We're not everything to everyone. We help users compare options to achieve outcomes. That's it. Be clear about what use cases and outcomes we're best suited to solve
- **Stories over features**: Show how Forma is being used and adding value. User stories are more powerful than feature lists
- **Community and Product Center as primary channels**: These are our owned channels that compound over time. Invest heavily here
- **Self-serve, not sales-led**: Marketing generates leads for self-serve channels through content and owned platforms
- **Speed of experimentation**: The team needs to move fast. Build for agility, not perfection

## How I expect you to work

**Own the narrative**: You're not just doing marketing activities - you're building and defending a point of view about outcome-based design. Every piece of content, every campaign, every message should reinforce what Forma is (and isn't).

**Build content engines, not campaigns**: One-off campaigns are useful but don't compound. Build systems that continuously produce content - user stories, product updates, podcasts, community-generated content. Think like a media company.

**Activate the community**: Community should become our loudest voice. Your job is to ensure they're enabled to share content about Forma online and offline. Work closely with Community (dotted line) to ensure aligned messaging and treat them as a core pillar of content strategy.

**Educate stakeholders**: Help Product, Engineering, and other teams understand what's required for PLG and self-serve success. Get them to support the operating model we need. This isn't optional - without their support, self-serve won't work.

**Leverage .com/next**: Build the relationship with the .com/next team. They've said we get access to the same functionality they have - make sure we're using it. This is a strategic relationship, not just a vendor.

**Experiment fast**: The team needs the ability to try things quickly. Build processes that enable speed, not bureaucracy.

## My role

- Provide strategic context on Growth priorities and where marketing fits
- Help navigate stakeholder alignment on PLG requirements
- Clear blockers when teams aren't supporting what we need
- Challenge whether we're truly owning the outcome-based design narrative
- Provide air cover for bold experiments

# Detailed expectations

### Own the outcome-based design narrative

**What**: Establish and defend Forma's position as the leader in outcome-based design.

**Key activities**:
- Develop clear, opinionated messaging about what Forma is and isn't
- Create content that educates the market on outcome-based design
- Address misconceptions (we're not a Rhino/SketchUp replacement)
- Define which use cases and outcomes we're best suited to solve

**Expectations**: Users should arrive at Forma knowing what it does - comparing options to achieve outcomes. If users are still arriving expecting a design tool, the narrative work isn't landing. Measure whether user expectations are shifting.

### Build a content engine that tells user stories

**What**: Create a system for continuously producing content that shows how Forma adds value to real users.

**Key activities**:
- Develop processes for capturing and telling user stories
- Create content formats that showcase outcome-based design in action
- Build platforms (podcasts, video series, etc.) that establish us as the voice of outcome-based design

**Expectations**: This isn't about occasional case studies. It's about a machine that continuously produces compelling content. User stories should be our primary content type - they're more credible and shareable than anything we write about ourselves.

### Make Community the #1 growth channel

**What**: Ensure Community becomes our primary channel for growth through user-generated content and advocacy.

**Key activities**:
- Work with Community (dotted line) to ensure aligned messaging
- Enable community members to share content about Forma online and offline
- Treat Community as a core pillar of content strategy
- Support Community in representing Forma in forums and events

**Expectations**: Community has its own approach - don't dictate how they work. Your job is to ensure they're integrated with overall content strategy and messaging. The goal is users becoming our voices at scale.

**Expectations**: Product Center should be competing with Community for our top channel. This is owned territory - we control the experience, the content, and the conversion paths. Invest accordingly.

# Operating principles

1. **Narrative first**: Everything we do reinforces the outcome-based design narrative. If content or campaigns don't support that position, question whether to do them.

2. **Users as voices**: Our most credible marketing is users telling their stories. Build systems that enable and amplify their voices rather than just creating our own content.

3. **Owned over rented**: Community and Product Center are ours. Invest in channels we control that compound over time.

4. **Self-serve mindset**: We're building for users who discover, evaluate, and convert themselves. Design content and experiences for that journey.

5. **Speed over perfection**: Experiment fast. Learn what works. Kill what doesn't. The team needs agility, not bureaucracy.

6. **Opinionated positioning**: We can't be everything to everyone. Be clear about what we do, what we don't do, and who we're for. Clarity beats broad appeal.
