# The destination

## Why we're doing this

First Strike is the key moment where users experience enough value to return and build habitual usage. Without a strong First Strike, users churn regardless of how good our marketing, sales, or onboarding UI might be. This is the most direct lever we have to drive retention and sustainable MAU growth.

Current metrics tell a clear story: only ~20% of users who enter a Site reach First Strike; 16% reach Site Design FSM on day 1. We're targeting 50% on day 1. This isn't incremental improvement territory - it requires transformational thinking about the user journey.

## What success looks like

- **A high-velocity experimentation engine**: 16+ experiments per quarter with documented learnings, 60-70% failure rate (because you're testing bold hypotheses, not safe bets)
- **A strong, well-prioritized backlog**: Experiments and initiatives framed from user problems, with clear root cause analysis and testable hypotheses
- **System-level insights**: Understanding not just whether users reach First Strike, but whether First Strike actually causes them to return (correlation vs causation)
- **Reusable patterns**: Onboarding, activation, and learning experiences that transfer across Site Design, Building Design, and future apps
- **Users who find value fast**: 50% of commercial new users in a site reach First Strike on day 1

## The focus

- **Narrow beats broad**: Solve specific problems for specific ideal users deeply, rather than building for everyone
- **Root cause over symptoms**: If users drop off, understand why at the deepest level - not just where in the funnel
- **Analysis is more than environmental**: Users need to compare scenarios and understand which approach is best - we don't enable that well today, and it may be blocking First Strike
- **The straight-line journey**: What's the fastest, most intuitive path from entry to First Strike? Every experiment should test an aspect of this

## How I expect you to work

**High autonomy, high alignment**: You own the experiments. You decide what to build, what to test, what to kill. But stay tightly aligned with Maria on priorities and the bigger business picture - leverage her PM experience and sense of urgency.

**Execution over perfection**: I want you to learn by doing. Sometimes you can overthink and delay when you should be building and learning. Bias toward action - a scrappy experiment that teaches something is better than a perfect plan that never ships.

**Decisions on the edges**: If the squad always agrees with your decisions, you're not pushing hard enough. Good PM judgment means making calls that are uncomfortable, especially when uncertainty is high. The higher the uncertainty, the more aggressively you should descope - build only what's needed to learn.

**Embrace failure as signal**: Target 60-70% of experiments failing. Help the squad internalize that most code will be thrown away - that's not waste, it's learning. When experiments fail, the insight is the win.

**Instinct matters**: Leveraging intuition is part of being a PM. Try things outside the primary scope if your gut says there's something there - but balance this with the core mission. Don't get distracted, but don't be afraid to explore.

## My role

- Clear the path: Remove blockers with Site Design, Contextual Data, Analysis squads
- Challenge your thinking: Push back when hypotheses feel weak or scope feels too broad
- Provide business context: Help you see how First Strike connects to MAU targets and commercial goals
- Protect the mission: Keep the squad focused when shiny objects appear

# Operating principles

1. **Outcome over consensus**: If everyone agrees, you're not pushing hard enough. Make the call, run the experiment, learn.

2. **Descope under uncertainty**: When you don't know if something will work, build the minimum needed to find out. More uncertainty = smaller scope.

3. **Code is temporary, learning is permanent**: Most experiments will be thrown away. Design for learning velocity, not production quality.

4. **Narrow focus, high impact**: Better to solve one user problem deeply than five problems shallowly. The more focused you are on a specific ideal user journey, the clearer the signal.

5. **Try things**: Focus doesn't mean rigid. If your instinct says there's something worth exploring outside the primary scope, try it - but balance with the core mission.

6. **Root cause or nothing**: Surface-level fixes don't compound. Keep asking why until you're at the real problem.
