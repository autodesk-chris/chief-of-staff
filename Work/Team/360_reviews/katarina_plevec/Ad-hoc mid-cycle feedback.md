I had like 5 minutes to think about this, so please take my input with a big grain of salt.**What she does well:**  
She’s great at proactively giving feedback, spotting gaps, and taking initiative. She brings deep PM expertise and regularly supports other PMs with thoughtful, hands-on help. She’s also excellent at challenging assumptions and asking, “Should we really do this?” Not afraid to push for better thinking when it matters.**Areas to grow:**  
Her communication style can sometimes come off as a bit too sharp or focused on making a point, especially when reflecting on decisions. There’s a subtle but important difference between suggesting a better approach and implying she could have done it better herself. The latter can make others feel small or dismissed, which undermines trust in her otherwise valuable input.Self-promotion is important, especially in a large company where people need to understand how your skills can be used. But it’s often more effective to show your strengths through your work than to talk about them directly. Self-promotion that leaves others feeling smaller or overlooked rarely lands well.Another area to be mindful of is using sub-forums to rally support for a viewpoint. It tends to create division rather than understanding. It’s more productive to ask clarifying questions, understand the current intent or context, and figure out how to improve things from there.**What could 10X her impact:**  
Be more curious about the bigger problems leaders are trying to solve and how she might contribute. Asking, “Where are the bottlenecks, and how could I help reduce them?” can be a powerful way to increase impact.The key is not just to surface problems, but to take ownership and solve them in a way that reduces work for others, not adds to it.
