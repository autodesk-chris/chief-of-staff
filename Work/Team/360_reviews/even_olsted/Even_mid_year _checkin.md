Advice / Actions

## 1. Data & Impact

Advice:

“Being great isn’t just about pulling insights from dashboards — it’s about connecting those insights to the bigger story of the product. At Forma, retention is king. Correlated metrics (like activation events, or feature adoption rates) are helpful signals, but they’re not the destination. A strong PM will always ask: ‘Did this experiment actually move retention?’ and trace the causal link.

- Great looks like: showing not just that adoption of Feature X went up 10%, but that those users retained at a higher rate after 3 months. That’s how you build credibility with leadership and earn the team’s trust that your work moves the business needle.”

---

## 2. Bigger, Bolder Bets

Advice:

“Iteration is safe — but great PMs balance safety with boldness. In the AEC space, architects don’t dream of a 2% improvement in workflows; they dream of radically faster, more confident design decisions. Sometimes that means proposing an experiment that feels a little uncomfortable.

- Great looks like: running one or two ‘swing-for-the-fences’ experiments each quarter. Not reckless, but bold — an experiment that, if it works, could materially change adoption or retention. For example, instead of tweaking a button flow, test a new onboarding path that rethinks how we introduce the value of Forma to architects in their first project. Even if it fails, the learning is big.”

---

## 3. Problem Framing & Hypotheses

Advice:

“As architects, we’re trained to sit with the problem before jumping into solutions. Great PMs do the same. It’s tempting to jump straight into designing the MVP spec, but the discipline is staying in the problem space longer.

- Great looks like: writing hypotheses in plain language about what user need or friction you believe exists and why solving it might move retention. For example: ‘We believe architects churn because they don’t see Forma’s value in the first week. If we can help them complete a meaningful design scenario in <30 minutes, retention will improve.’ That’s a crisp, problem-first hypothesis that keeps the team aligned.” 

---

## 4. Confidence & Product Opinion

Advice:

“Stakeholder input is gold, but too much can water things down. Great PMs listen deeply but then synthesize and make a call. Architects are used to doing this all the time — you gather input from structural engineers, clients, contractors, but at the end of the day, you hold the pen on the design.

- Great looks like: thanking stakeholders for their input, but then stating your product POV clearly: ‘Based on the data and what we heard from users, I think our next step should be X.’ That clarity builds trust, even if some disagree. People would rather follow a clear opinion than a diluted consensus.” 

---

## 5. Strategic Roadmap

Advice:

“Ad hoc experiments can give you wins, but they won’t get you to PM excellence. Great PMs carve out a roadmap that ties squad work to long-term company goals. At Forma, that means showing how today’s experiments ladder up to retention and adoption over quarters, not just weeks.

- Great looks like: building a lightweight quarterly roadmap that balances quick experiments with 1–2 strategic initiatives. Something like: ‘This quarter, our goal is to reduce churn in the first 30 days. We’ll run A/B tests on onboarding flows, but we’ll also invest in a strategic bet — helping users get their BIM model into Forma faster.’ That roadmap helps the squad and leadership see the bigger picture.”

---

## 6. Expanding Influence

Advice:

“Great PMs don’t just lift their own squad — they help raise the bar across the org. At Forma, experimentation is still maturing. You can add real value by sharing learnings and frameworks with other squads.

- Great looks like: running a short brown bag on ‘How we’re tying experiments to retention outcomes’ or sharing a playbook on writing better hypotheses. That’s how you start moving from being a good squad PM to being a company-level impact player.”
