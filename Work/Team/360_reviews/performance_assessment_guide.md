# Performance Assessment Guide


**Outcome-Based Assessment with OKR-Informed Analysis and One ORBIT Behaviors**

---

## 1. Purpose of This Guide

This guide supports consistent and evidence-based performance assessment by focusing on:

- **WHAT outcomes were delivered**, based on self-reviews and feedback
    
- **HOW those outcomes were achieved**, using One ORBIT behaviors
    

Team OKRs provide **strategic context** for assessment, but individuals are **not expected to explicitly reference OKRs** in their self-reviews. Reviewers use OKRs to **interpret, validate, and contextualize outcomes**, not to score performance.

---

## 2. Performance Framework Overview

Performance assessment is structured around two core dimensions, with OKRs used as an **interpretive lens**.

|Dimension|Core Question|Primary Evidence|
|---|---|---|
|**WHAT**|What outcomes did the individual deliver?|Self-reviews, feedback, observable results|
|**HOW**|How was the work done?|One ORBIT behaviors, feedback, examples|

**OKRs inform the analysis of WHAT**, but do not replace outcome-based evaluation.

---

## 3. Assessment Structure: CARE Method (Reviewer Use)

All assessments should be structured using the CARE method, even if the employee did not explicitly do so.

|CARE Element|Reviewer Focus|
|---|---|
|**Context**|What priorities or problems was the work addressing?|
|**Actions**|What actions did the individual take?|
|**Results**|What outcomes or progress were achieved?|
|**Experiences**|What behaviors, skills, and values were demonstrated (One ORBIT)?|

---

## 4. Identifying and Validating Outcomes (WHAT)

### 4.1 Extract Outcomes from Inputs

Reviewers begin by identifying **outcomes described or implied** in:

- Self-reviews
    
- Peer, partner, and manager feedback
    

Outcomes may include:

- Delivery of initiatives or milestones
    
- Removal of blockers or dependencies
    
- Improvements to speed, quality, or effectiveness
    
- Customer, stakeholder, or team impact
    
- Enablement of others’ success
    

Activities alone are not outcomes unless they clearly led to impact.

---

### 4.2 Interpret Outcomes Through the Lens of OKRs

After outcomes are identified, reviewers consider team OKRs to answer:

- Which OKR(s) does this outcome most naturally support?
    
- Which Key Result(s) does it align with or reinforce?
    
- Is the contribution **direct**, **supporting**, or **enabling**?
    

This mapping is **analytical and reviewer-driven**.  
Lack of explicit OKR reference in a self-review is acceptable.

---

## 5. Using Feedback to Reinforce or Challenge Outcomes

For each meaningful outcome, reviewers should triangulate:

|Input|Purpose|
|---|---|
|Self-review|Claimed impact and intent|
|Feedback|Independent reinforcement or alternative perspective|
|OKRs|Strategic relevance of the outcome|

### Signals of Strong Reinforcement

- Feedback references similar outcomes or impact
    
- Multiple sources describe consistent value
    
- Outcomes clearly support team priorities reflected in OKRs
    

### Signals to Examine Further

- Claimed outcomes not visible in feedback
    
- Feedback highlights different contributions
    
- Outcomes appear disconnected from team priorities
    

---

## 6. Assessing HOW the Work Was Done (One ORBIT)

Once outcomes are validated, assess **HOW** the individual worked while delivering them.

Behavioral assessment should:

- Use **specific examples** from feedback and observation
    
- Reference One ORBIT dimensions as lenses, not checklists
    
- Focus on **patterns of behavior**, not isolated moments
    

Key question:

> Did the individual’s behaviors strengthen the team’s ability to deliver outcomes aligned with our priorities?

---

## 7. Final Written Output: Outcome-Focused, OKR-Aligned

### Output Expectations

The final assessment output should:

- Be written in **outcome-oriented language**
    
- Reference **OKR alignment explicitly but lightly**
    
- Avoid framing performance as OKR completion
    
- Clearly separate **outcomes (WHAT)** from **behaviors (HOW)**
    

### Recommended Outcome Statement Format

> **Outcome:**  
> _[Describe the outcome and its impact]_
> 
> **Alignment:**  
> _This outcome aligns well with KR[ X ] under OKR[ Y ] by [brief explanation]._

### Example

> **Outcome:**  
> Improved cross-functional delivery speed by proactively unblocking dependencies and aligning stakeholders, contributing to faster execution on priority initiatives.
> 
> **Alignment:**  
> This aligns well with KR2 (Reduce cycle time for cross-team delivery) by accelerating handoffs and reducing delays across dependent teams.

---

## 8. Bringing WHAT and HOW Together

A complete assessment should clearly articulate:

- **WHAT outcomes were delivered**, grounded in evidence and reinforced by feedback
    
- **HOW those outcomes were achieved**, using One ORBIT behaviors
    

### Example Summary

> Delivered meaningful improvements in cross-team execution by removing blockers and improving alignment, supporting faster progress on priority initiatives. This impact was achieved through strong One Autodesk collaboration and Relentless focus on execution, consistently reinforced by partner feedback.
